Scanning a document

  1. Add your pages. Choose Use camera and press the shutter once per page — you can take several in a row without stopping. Or bring in images you already have — choose them with the Choose images button, or drop them onto the dashed area.
  2. Set the corners. Drag the four handles to the corners of the page in the picture. Local Scan straightens whatever is inside them into a proper rectangle, so a photo taken at an angle still comes out square.
  3. Pick a look. Document is usually what you want for printed text. Greyscale keeps shading. Original leaves the colours alone.
  4. Put the pages in order. Use the up and down buttons on each row. On a larger screen you can also drag a row by the handle at its left.
  5. Choose a format and save. PDF, Word, plain text, Markdown or individual images — pick one, adjust whatever settings it offers, and save. There is also a Copy the text button that puts the words straight on your clipboard.

The camera won't start.

A browser will only hand the camera to a page served over https:// (or on localhost). Beyond that, the usual causes are that permission was declined for this site, or another app or tab already has the camera. Local Scan names the cause when the browser reports one, and importing photos you have already taken always works regardless.

Which image formats can I bring in?

JPEG and PNG work everywhere, and most other common image formats — WebP, GIF and AVIF among them — open wherever your browser can decode them. HEIC and HEIF files — what an iPhone produces by default — only open if your browser can decode them, which today mainly means Safari on Apple devices. If a file can't be opened, Local Scan says so rather than failing quietly. Converting to JPEG first always works.

Document mode made a photograph look wrong.

Document mode assumes it is looking at ink on paper: it works out how bright the paper is in each part of the picture and lifts it to white. A large area of even mid-grey — the sky in a photograph, a block of shading — reads as paper to it and goes white. For anything that isn't a printed page, use Greyscale or Original.

Which quality should I choose?

Balanced suits most scans. Smaller file is fine when you only need to read the text. Lossless (PNG) pairs well with the Document look, because the sharp black-and-white edges it produces are exactly what JPEG compression handles worst.

What does "fit to image" mean?

Page size is a PDF setting. Each PDF page takes the shape of its own picture, so nothing is cropped or padded. A PDF page also has a physical size, and Local Scan works that out by treating the picture as a 150 dpi scan — so a full page photographed close up comes out at roughly the size of the real thing. Choose A4 or US Letter instead if the file has to print on standard paper; each page is then fitted and centred, in whichever orientation matches the picture.

What are Word, Text and Markdown reading from?

Those formats, and the Picture + searchable text PDF, need the words off the page rather than a picture of them. Local Scan reads them with a text recogniser that runs on your device — the first time you save one of those formats, or copy the text, it fetches the recogniser (about 6 MB, once) from the same place the page came from, and after that it works offline. Your pages are never part of that. This version reads English, and does best on sharp, upright pages; if a page comes back empty, the Document look under Adjust page usually helps.

What is a searchable PDF?

The same page picture you would get otherwise, with the recognised words laid underneath it — invisible, but selectable, copyable and findable with your reader's search. The words usually sit under the printed ones, close enough to highlight; when a page gives back no positions they are still there to search, just not under their own glyphs. Recognition is never perfect either, so treat it as a good search index rather than a perfect transcript.

I already bought Pro. How do I get it back?

Pro is sold only in the iPhone and iPad app, and it belongs to the Apple Account that bought it. On a new device, or after reinstalling, open the app, choose one of the text formats, and tap Restore purchases on the panel that appears — signed in with that same Apple Account. Can I get a refund?

Apple handles refunds for everything bought through the App Store, Local Scan Pro included — we cannot process one ourselves. Ask Apple at reportaproblem.apple.com, signed in with the Apple Account that made the purchase.

Can I use it offline?

Yes. Once the page has loaded once, it keeps working with no connection at all — including the camera, the processing, and saving. The one exception is the text recogniser behind Word, Text, Markdown and searchable PDF: it has to load once while you are connected, and then it too works offline.

Yes, from the first moment. Everything the app runs came with it, the text recogniser included, so the camera, the processing, the reading and the saving all work in Airplane Mode. Pro works offline too once you own it. Buying or restoring it is the one thing that needs a connection, because that part is Apple's.

Can I do it all from the keyboard?

Yes. Tab moves through every control. On a corner handle the arrow keys nudge it, and holding Shift moves it further. The up and down buttons on each page row reorder pages without dragging.
